Hanging rail height in a walk-in wardrobe

Rail height is the one dimension people get wrong and then live with for years. Too low and coats drag on the bottom shelf; too high and you are standing on a stool to fetch a shirt. There are only a few sensible heights, and which one you use depends on what is hanging under the rail and what is above it.

Start from the garment, not from the carcass

Every rail height is really a garment length plus clearance. A shirt or a folded pair of trousers on a hanger occupies roughly the top third of a wardrobe; a winter coat or a long dress needs most of the height of the room. So decide first whether a column is double hanging (two short rails stacked) or long hanging (one rail high up with the full drop below it), and the number follows.

Measure to the centre of the rail, from the finished floor. Working from the bottom panel of the carcass instead is the classic way to end up 118 mm (4 5/8 in) out — the plinth and the bottom panel are not part of the hanging space.

Double hanging: 1000 mm and 2000 mm

Double hanging doubles the capacity of a column for shirts, blouses, jackets and trousers folded over the bar. The lower rail sits at 1000 mm (39 3/8 in) and the upper at 2000 mm (78 3/4 in). That leaves roughly 900 mm (35 7/16 in) of clear drop under the lower rail and a full metre under the upper one — enough for anything that is not a coat — and it puts the top rail exactly at the reach limit.

The rail runs the full width inside the carcass, so a 600 mm (23 5/8 in) unit gives 564 mm (22 3/16 in) of rail per level once the two 18 mm (23/32 in) sides are taken off. Budget about 25–30 mm (1 – 1 3/16 in) of rail per garment — a shirt on a wire hanger at the bottom of that, a jacket on a shaped hanger at the top — and a 600 mm unit holds roughly twenty things per level. Coats and thick knitwear compress that quickly, so count a long-hanging column at half.

Long hanging: 1600 to 1800 mm

A single rail for coats, suit bags and long dresses goes at 1600–1800 mm (63 – 70 7/8 in). Use the lower end if the column also has a shoe shelf or a drawer stack at the bottom, and the upper end if the drop below the rail is clear to the bottom panel. Above 1800 mm (70 7/8 in) you are buying hanging length you cannot reach comfortably; below 1600 mm (63 in) a full-length coat starts to touch down.

The 2000 mm ceiling on reach

Walk-in Planner clamps every rail to a maximum of 2000 mm (78 3/4 in) above the floor. If you give a hanging zone more height than that — for instance a single hanging zone that runs to the top of a 2250 mm (88 9/16 in) carcass — the rail stays at 2000 mm and the space above it becomes open headroom, dimensioned on the elevation. That space is not wasted: it is where a suitcase or a seasonal box goes, on a shelf you add yourself later.

Clearances the rail needs

Both clearances a rail needs are vertical; there is nothing to keep clear at the ends, because the rail simply spans the carcass. It hangs 80 mm (3 1/8 in) below the underside of the shelf above, which is what a hanger hook needs to swing over the bar without scraping. And wherever you put it, it has to stay at least 40 mm (1 9/16 in) clear of the top and the bottom of its own hanging zone, so a loaded hanger can be lifted straight off rather than dragged out. Set a rail closer than that to the top or the bottom of its zone and the planner flags it as an error instead of drawing it.

FAQ

How high should a wardrobe hanging rail be?

For a single rail holding coats and long dresses, 1600 to 1800 mm above the floor. For double hanging, put the lower rail at 1000 mm and the upper one at 2000 mm. Measure to the centre of the rail, from the finished floor, not from the bottom panel of the carcass.

Why does the planner cap the rail at 2000 mm?

Because that is roughly the height at which most adults stop being able to lift a loaded hanger off a rail without reaching over their head. In a tall zone the planner parks the rail at 2000 mm and leaves the space above it as open headroom, which the drawings dimension, rather than pushing the rail up to the top of the carcass where nothing could reach it.

How much clearance does a rail need?

Both of them are vertical. The rail hangs 80 mm below the underside of the shelf above, so a hanger hook can pass over the bar, and it has to stay at least 40 mm clear of the top and the bottom of its own hanging zone so a loaded hanger can be lifted straight off. Along its length the rail simply spans the inside of the carcass, so there is no end clearance to allow for.
